Diary, 1941

Bernard Edward Shambrook
NX40242

The First World War record linked to Bernard lists him as Private 2076, 5th Light Horse Regiment, 14th Reinforcements, joining on 18 October 1915. [8] The regiment belonged to the 2nd Light Horse Brigade, part of the ANZAC Mounted Division from 1916. [21] Virtual War Memorial Australia links this record to NX40242; that connection awaits confirmation from his service files. [9]

Bernard kept this notebook during his Australian Army service in the Second World War. It follows his April 1941 voyage aboard Queen Elizabeth, from Sydney through Fremantle and Trincomalee to Suez, then his journey into Palestine. He records life on the ship, the canal and railway journeys, and later movements between camps.

Alongside the diary entries are tent and duty rolls, soldiers’ names and addresses, weapon and routine notes, Arabic words, currency calculations, and sketches of Suez and the cemeteries at Gaza and Jerusalem. He also copied names and inscriptions commemorating soldiers of the First World War.

The dated travel and service entries run from April to August 1941. One Gaza heading may read June or September. The 1917–18 dates in the cemetery lists refer to the men remembered there.

Background and records

The Department of Veterans’ Affairs records Bernard Edward Shambrook, NX40242, as born on 1 September 1900 in Hertfordshire, England. He enlisted at Tamworth on 18 June 1940, from Wee Waa, with Winifred Shambrook as his next of kin. He was discharged on 23 October 1941 as a private in HQ Guard Battalion. [6]

His service number appears in the notebook’s Tent 17 roster beside “A/Sgt SHAMBROOK B.E.” — acting sergeant. The DVA index gives his substantive rank at discharge. [1][6]

An earlier AWM embarkation roll names Bernard Edward Shambrook, 2076, a 19-year-old butcher from Pimlico, Townsville, in the 5th Light Horse Regiment’s 14th Reinforcements. Virtual War Memorial Australia links both numbers. This is a strong lead; the two service files have not yet been compared. [8][7][9]
