Records

The Department of Veterans’ Affairs records Bernard Edward Shambrook, NX40242, as born on 1 September 1900 in Hertfordshire, England. He enlisted at Tamworth on 18 June 1940, from Wee Waa, with Winifred Shambrook as his next of kin. He was discharged on 23 October 1941 as a private in HQ Guard Battalion. [6]

His service number appears in the notebook’s Tent 17 roster beside “A/Sgt SHAMBROOK B.E.” — acting sergeant. The DVA index gives his substantive rank at discharge. [1][6]

An earlier AWM embarkation roll names Bernard Edward Shambrook, 2076, a 19-year-old butcher from Pimlico, Townsville, in the 5th Light Horse Regiment’s 14th Reinforcements. Virtual War Memorial Australia links both numbers. This is a strong lead; the two service files have not yet been compared. [8][7][9]

Service

  1. 18 October 1915

    2076 · Private · 14th Reinforcements

    The 5th Light Horse roll records Bernard Edward Shambrook joining on this date, aged 19, a butcher from Pimlico, Townsville. His mother Elizabeth was his next of kin. [8]

  2. 18 June 1940

    NX40242 · Army enlistment

    The WWII nominal roll records enlistment at Tamworth, NSW, from Wee Waa. [6]

  3. 11 April 1941

    Queen Elizabeth voyage

    The notebook records leaving Sydney aboard Queen Elizabeth, then travelling through Fremantle and Trincomalee to Suez. [3]

  4. June 12 · year inferred as 1941

    Guard Battalion entry

    The notebook records marching into “1st Aust Corps Guard Btn” from “16 ITB Special wing”. June 12 appears beside a battalion-order reference; the precise transfer date remains uncertain. [4]

  5. Undated notebook roster

    A/Sgt Shambrook B.E.

    The Tent 17 roster gives A/Sgt Shambrook B.E.; the ownership inscription says Sgt. [1][2]

  6. 23 October 1941

    Private · HQ Guard Battalion

    DVA records discharge as a private, with HQ Guard Battalion as his final posting. [6]

Birth details

The birth details differ: DVA gives 1 September 1900 in Herts (Hertfordshire); the Virtual War Memorial profile gives 1 September 1896 in Herefordshire. The 1915 roll records age 19. The reason for the difference is unresolved. [6][9][8]

Context

Convoy US.10

AWM photographs identify Queen Elizabeth and Mauretania leaving Sydney in April 1941 in convoy US.10, with Île de France and Nieuw Amsterdam also pictured. The official naval history lists those four transports and Queen Mary. [10][18][19]

Photograph IMG_0911

The canal transfer

A 1982 Pioneer Battalions Association history names SS Ethiopia on the Port Tewfik–Kantara transfer in May 1941. It supports the spelling used in the reading copy of Bernard’s canal journey. [20][3]

Photograph IMG_0910 · Photograph IMG_0911

7th Australian General Hospital

AWM photograph 022693 places the 7th Australian General Hospital at Kafr Balu, near Rehovot, helping locate the notebook entry. [11][3]

Photograph IMG_0911

Guard Battalion

The AWM holds a unit history for August 1940–October 1941 and a May–June 1941 diary under AWM52 8/4/1. The catalogue entries have been checked; the document pages remain to be examined for Bernard. [12][13][4]

Photograph IMG_0913

Cemetery notes

The Gaza pages bring together a 1941 heading, soldiers’ names and dates from 1917–18, and a sketch of the LIV East Anglian Division memorial. The heading’s month could be June or September. [5]

Service files

The complete personnel files would help resolve the birth details, the link between the two service numbers, and his rank history. Neither individual dossier has yet been retrieved.
